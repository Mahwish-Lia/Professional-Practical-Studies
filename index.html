Guiding question

One question organises everything here

Comprehensive action competence — berufliche Handlungskompetenz — is understood through its four dimensions: professional (Fachkompetenz), methodological (Methodenkompetenz), social (Sozialkompetenz) and individual competence (Individualkompetenz). The portfolio asks where each dimension was built, which boundaries between academic knowing and professional doing appeared along the way, and what carried the crossing. Reflection

The boundaries crossed

The answer to the guiding question, condensed. Each boundary appeared in the work documented above; each was crossed by a specific move — and it is the move, not the achievement, that shows the transition.

Holding a competence vs. teaching it

Writing academically myself was tacit; teaching others to write required making that competence explicit and decomposable. Crossed by breaking the writing process into workshop stages with feedback at each.

Innovation vs. scientific rigour

The VET 6.0 seminar was designed for innovation without designing in rigour — the evaluation named it. Crossed by redesigning the academic writing course as the implemented answer to that critique.

Designing a course vs. getting it adopted

Academic study teaches curriculum design, not curriculum politics. Crossed by taking the AI in TVET initiative to the chair and carrying it from idea to launch.

Using digital tools vs. building with them

Consuming platforms is not the same as authoring infrastructure. Crossed by adopting course-as-code — LiaScript authored, GitHub delivered, open source, zero cost.

Using AI vs. being qualified to teach it

Familiarity with AI tools is not a teaching qualification. Crossed by completing the certification pathway myself before introducing it to learners.

Claiming learning vs. evidencing it

A course can assert that learning happened, or it can produce proof. Crossed by designing verifiable outputs — certificates, microcredentials, capstone chapters — into the courses themselves.
